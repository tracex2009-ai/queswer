II. Read the questions given below and share your answers with your classmates and teacher.

1. Which language(s) do your grandparents or elderly relatives speak?
Answer: My grandparents mainly speak Hindi and our regional mother tongue at home. They can also understand basic English words used in daily life.


2. How do they spend their time? How do you spend time with them?
Answer: They spend their time reading the newspaper, praying, gardening, and talking with neighbours. I spend time with them by listening to their childhood stories, helping them use a smartphone, and playing board games like Ludo or chess.


3. What is your favourite experience with them?
Answer: My favourite experience is sitting with my grandmother in the evening while she tells me traditional folk stories and shares memories from her school days.


4. What is something that the elderly in your family cannot do easily but enjoy watching you do?
Answer: My grandparents find it difficult to use modern smartphones, video-calling apps, or ride a bicycle fast, but they love watching me use technology easily and play outdoor sports.

I. Complete the cause and effect table given below based on Part I of the story. Share your answers with your classmates and teacher.

Cause Effect
1. The transport system in the north Karnataka village was not very good. (i) The morning papers arrived late in the day and weekly magazines would come one day late.
2. The grandmother, Krishtakka, never went to school. (ii) She could not read or write and had to depend on her granddaughter to read the story to her.
3. The story Kashi Yatre had a strong emotional and spiritual appeal that connected with her wish to visit Kashi. (iii) She was deeply interested in the story and discussed it with her friends.
4. The narrator went to a wedding for a week. (iv) Nobody was there to read the next episode of Kashi Yatre to the grandmother, which made her feel helpless and sad.
5. She lost her mother when she was young, and in those days people did not consider education essential for girls. (v) The grandmother never went to school.
6. The grandmother regretted not going to school. (vi) She realized the importance of being independent and decided to learn how to read and write.

II. Do you think the narrator expected to see her grandmother in tears when she returned to the village? If yes, why? If no, why not?

Answer: No, the narrator did not expect to see her grandmother in tears. She had never seen her grandmother cry before, even in the most difficult situations, so seeing her helpless and crying came as a complete shock to the narrator.

III. How might the narrator help her grandmother to fulfil her desire to learn to read and write?

Answer: The narrator can help her grandmother by becoming her teacher and giving her daily lessons with patience and encouragement. She can teach her the Kannada alphabet step by step, give her reading and writing practice, and help her build confidence so she can read on her own.

Critical Reflection

I. Read the extracts given below and answer the questions that follow.

Extract 1

"When I came back to my village, I saw my grandmother in tears. I was surprised, for I had never seen her cry even in the most difficult situations. What had happened? I was worried.
‘Avva, is everything all right? Are you okay?’
I used to call her Avva, which means mother in the Kannada spoken in north Karnataka.
She nodded but did not reply. I did not understand and forgot about it. In the night, after dinner, we were sleeping in the open terrace of our house. It was a summer night and there was a full moon. Avva came and sat next to me. Her affectionate hands touched my forehead."

(i) Complete the following sentence with the appropriate option.
The phrase 'never seen her cry in the most difficult situations' tells us that the grandmother was ______.
Answer: A. strong-willed

(ii) Complete the following with the correct option from those given in the brackets.
Grandmother did not reply when the narrator asked if she was alright because she might have been too emotional (emotional/tired) to respond.

(iii) Identify the clue from the extract that indicates a rural setting with traditional customs.
Answer: Calling the grandmother 'Avva' (meaning mother in north Karnataka Kannada) and sleeping together on the open terrace of the house at night indicate a rural setting with traditional customs.

(iv) Which lines of the extract establish a tender atmosphere?
Answer: The lines "It was a summer night and there was a full moon. Avva came and sat next to me. Her affectionate hands touched my forehead." establish a tender and loving atmosphere.

(v) Which of the following aspect is NOT emphasised in the given extract?
Answer: C. the grandmother's regret over her lack of education

Extract 2

"‘I have decided I want to learn the Kannada alphabet from tomorrow onwards. I will work very hard. I will keep Saraswati Puja day during Dassara as the deadline. That day I should be able to read a novel on my own. I want to be independent.’
I saw the determination on her face. Yet I laughed at her.
‘Avva, at this age of sixty-two you want to learn the alphabet? All your hair is grey, your hands are wrinkled, you wear spectacles and you work so much in the kitchen…’
Childishly I made fun of the old lady. But she just smiled.
‘For a good cause if you are determined, you can overcome any obstacle. I will work harder than anybody but I will do it. For learning there is no age bar.’"

(i) What does the grandmother's statement, "I want to be independent," reveal about her character?
Answer: B. She desires self-sufficiency.

(ii) Fill in the blank with the appropriate option from those given in the brackets.
The grandmother's determination shows that learning has no age limit (age limit/gender bias/cultural barriers).

(iii) Complete the following with a suitable reason.
The narrator laughs at her grandmother's decision to learn the alphabet at the age of sixty-two because she childishly felt that her grandmother was too old, had wrinkled hands, weak eyesight, and too much kitchen work to start learning.

(iv) List any two qualities displayed by the grandmother.
Answer: 1. Strong determination and willpower    2. Patience and self-confidence

(v) How can we say that the narrator is making assumptions about her grandmother?
Answer: The narrator assumes that because her grandmother is sixty-two years old, wears spectacles, and stays busy with household chores, she would not have the energy or ability to learn something new.

II. Answer the following questions.

1. Why do you think the grandmother felt embarrassed to ask someone else to read to her while the narrator was away?
Answer: The grandmother was an elderly and respected woman in the village. She felt ashamed and helpless admitting to outsiders that she could not read, as she did not want others to know about her illiteracy.

2. Why does the narrator initially laugh at her grandmother's determination to learn at the age of sixty-two?
Answer: Being only twelve years old, the narrator childishly thought that learning the alphabet was only meant for young children. She looked at her grandmother's grey hair, wrinkled hands, and busy kitchen routine, and doubted that she could study at that age.

3. What significance does the story of Kashi Yatre have in both the grandmother's life and the story?
Answer: In the grandmother's life, Kashi Yatre represented her own unfulfilled dream of visiting Kashi, which made her deeply attached to the story. In the main story, missing an episode of Kashi Yatre became the turning point that made her realize the pain of dependence and inspired her to become literate.

4. What does the grandmother's desire to learn the Kannada alphabet reflect about her?
Answer: It reflects her strong willpower, self-respect, and progressive mindset. She refused to let her age become an excuse and proved that anyone can achieve their goal with dedication and hard work.

5. What lessons can we infer from the grandmother's action of touching the narrator's feet?
Answer: By touching her young granddaughter's feet, the grandmother taught us humility, gratitude, and true respect for knowledge. She showed that a teacher deserves the highest respect regardless of their age or gender.

6. What does the following line tell us about the broader theme of the story?
'For a good cause if you are determined, you can overcome any obstacle.'
Answer: This line highlights the central theme of the story—that age, physical limitations, or past disadvantages cannot stop a person who has strong determination and a clear goal. With sincere effort, any challenge can be overcome.

7. How effectively does the story highlight the value of education in supporting personal independence?
Answer: The story very effectively shows how illiteracy makes even a capable adult feel helpless and dependent on others. Once the grandmother learns to read, she regains her confidence and freedom, proving that education empowers a person to live with dignity and self-reliance.

Speaking Activity

I. Turncoat Debate: Choose your topic and speak 'for' and 'against' for not more than one minute each.

Topic 1: It is important to learn a new language apart from your mother tongue.

For the Topic:
To begin with, I would like to speak 'for' the topic. My first argument in favour of learning a new language is that it opens doors to better education and global career opportunities. Most importantly, I want to mention that knowing multiple languages helps us connect with people from different cultures and improves our brain power and memory. In addition to that, when we travel to new places, speaking the local or global language makes us confident and independent.

Against the Topic:
On the contrary, there are two sides to this topic. I am pretty sure that forcing students to learn too many languages can create unnecessary academic pressure and distract them from core subjects like science, maths, and coding. Moreover, with modern translation apps and AI tools easily available on every smartphone today, communication across languages is already possible without spending years learning a new language. So, to put it in a few words, mastering one's mother tongue and key practical skills is far more important.

Topic 2: Learning can happen only when you are young.

For the Topic:
To begin with, I strongly feel that childhood and youth are the best stages for learning. During a young age, our brain is sharper, grasps new concepts faster, and adapts easily to new skills. Furthermore, young students have fewer family and work responsibilities, allowing them to focus completely on their studies.

Against the Topic:
On the other hand, I am not sure whether you can really put an age limit on learning. As we saw in the story How I Taught My Grandmother to Read, Avva learned the Kannada alphabet at the age of sixty-two through sheer determination. Adults bring life experience, patience, and self-motivation to their learning. Therefore, learning is a lifelong journey that can happen at any age.

Writing Task

I. Write a letter to the Editor of a local newspaper emphasising the importance of student participation in adult literacy camps.

B-42, Green Park Extension
New Delhi – 110016

25 November 2026

The Editor
The Times of India
New Delhi

Subject: Promoting Student Participation in Adult Literacy Camps

Sir/Madam,

As a concerned citizen, I would like to draw your attention towards the urgent need for student involvement in adult literacy programmes across our city. Despite rapid technological progress, a large section of elderly citizens and underprivileged adults still struggle to read, write, and perform basic digital tasks.

The issue at hand affects millions of adults who feel dependent on others even for simple tasks like reading signs, prescriptions, or bank forms. It would be beneficial if school and college students actively volunteer in adult literacy camps organised by NGOs and community centres. Such initiatives nurture a sense of social responsibility, empathy, and leadership among young learners while giving adults the confidence and dignity of self-reliance.

A possible solution to this issue could be introducing community teaching hours as part of school co-curricular activities and organising weekend literacy drives in local neighbourhoods. Schools can also use social media and morning assemblies to spread awareness and inspire more students to join this noble cause. I hope this letter gets published in the columns of your esteemed daily so that more youth come forward to build a fully literate society.

Yours truly,
Aarav Sharma
