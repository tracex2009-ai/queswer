NCERT Class 9 English Chapter 2 (Poem) 2 Gifts Of Grace : Honouring Our Vocations Question & Answers

Reflect and Respond

I. Given below are four riddles. Read and identify who these people are.

  1. In furrows deep, secrets I sow,
    As time passes,
    I watch them grow.
    Who am I? Farmer
  2. From wheel to kiln, my skill is born,
    Step by step, an art takes form.
    Who am I? Potter
  3. I lay foundations, brick by brick,
    To build a house, it’s me you pick.
    Who am I? Mason
  4. I work with pots, pans, and spice,
    Creating dishes that taste so nice.
    Who am I? Cook / Chef

II. What is the role and relevance of the people you identified in the riddles. How do they contribute to the society? Discuss with your classmates and teacher.

Answer: Each of these workers plays an essential role in sustaining our daily lives and building society:

  • Farmer: Cultivates crops and provides food grains, fruits, and vegetables that nourish the entire population.
  • Potter: Crafts earthen pots, utensils, and decorative clay items, preserving traditional art and offering eco-friendly household goods.
  • Mason: Builds strong foundations, houses, schools, and buildings, providing safe shelter and infrastructure.
  • Cook / Chef: Prepares nutritious and delicious meals that keep people healthy and bring communities together through food culture.

Check Your Understanding

I. Based on your understanding of the poem, state whether the following statements are true or false. Also, rectify the false statements.

  1. The poem highlights the skilled work of craftsperson.
    Answer: True
  2. The poet shares that musicians express emotions through their instruments.
    Answer: True
  3. The carpenters in the poem are admired for their logical work.
    Answer: False. Rectification: The carpenters in the poem are admired for creating things out of wood with mathematical precision.
  4. The electricians in the poem are recognised for their crucial role in lighting up lives.
    Answer: True
  5. The poem pays homage to shoemakers who manufacture quality footwear.
    Answer: True
  6. The poem celebrates the patriotism of the people of Bharat.
    Answer: False. Rectification: The poem celebrates the varied vocations, skills, and dignity of labour of the people of Bharat.
  7. The poet feels that each vocation deserves to be respected.
    Answer: True

II. Let us appreciate the poem.

  1. Rhyme Scheme and Lineation
    1. Does the poem strictly adhere to a rhyme scheme, or is it in free verse?
      Answer: The poem does not strictly adhere to a rhyme scheme; it is written in free verse.
    2. What is the impact of the varying length of lines in the poem?
      Answer: The varying length of lines creates a natural, conversational flow that mirrors the diversity, rhythm, and unique pace of different workers and their vocations.
    3. What is the pattern in the structure of most lines of the poem?
      Answer: Most lines follow a parallel, descriptive structure that introduces a specific group of workers followed by their actions and unique contributions.
  2. Speaker
    1. Who appears to be the speaker and what is her/his role here?
      Answer: The speaker appears to be an attentive observer or the poet who listens to, appreciates, and celebrates the everyday work and contributions of all workers across Bharat.
  3. Tone and Mood
    1. Fill in the blanks with suitable options from the box given below.
      serious | celebratory | hostile | reverential | joyful | sympathetic
      1. The tone is celebratory and reverential, depicting a sense of admiration and respect for the artisans and craftspersons.
      2. There is a joyful mood throughout the poem, capturing the vibrancy and richness of cultural traditions and skills.
  4. Imagery
    1. Select any two descriptions from the poem that evoke visual images.
      Answer:
      • "their craft, woven with colours and myriad hues"
      • "The boatmen gathering their nets from the shore, sailing"
    2. Fill in the blanks with suitable phrases.
      The poem includes auditory imagery through mentions of artisans with lutes, electricians humming, and boatmen singing while at work (or the delicious singing of the cook), emphasising the sounds associated with each vocation.
  5. Metaphor
    1. State whether the following sentence is true or false.
      The mention of ‘delicious singing’ of the cook is a metaphor because it implies that the quality of the singing is so enjoyable or pleasing that it can be equated to the experience of tasting something delicious.
      Answer: True
  6. Personification
    1. Select the line that tells us that the poet personifies vocations by attributing human-like qualities to them.
      Answer: “The voice of their vocation is the voice of their identity.” (Also: “I hear Bharat celebrating, the varied vocations I hear”)
  7. Repetition
    1. Why might the poet have begun and ended with the same line—‘I hear Bharat celebrating, the varied vocations I hear!’?
      Answer: The poet begins and ends with the same line to frame the poem, emphasize the central theme of honouring all professions, and create a musical, unified impression that every vocation contributes to the spirit of Bharat.
  8. Alliteration
    1. Identify two examples of alliteration from the poem.
      Answer:
      • “varied vocations” (repetition of the 'v' sound)
      • “work, work with cables and wires” / “sailing, and singing” (repetition of the 'w' and 's' sounds)
  9. Symbolism
    1. Each vocation in the poem symbolises something more than just a job. What does it symbolise?
      Answer: Each vocation symbolises a person's dignity, creativity, self-expression, identity, and vital contribution to the cultural and social fabric of the nation.

Critical Reflection

I. Read the extract given below and answer the questions that follow.

The shoemakers affirming the quality of their work, for the feet that walk, dance, run, jump, return home.
The delicious singing of the cook, or the rhythm of designer, mason, each celebrating what belongs to them and to none else,
  • The poet says that the shoemakers ‘affirm’ the quality of their work. What does ‘affirm’ refer to here?

  • What do quality shoes help with, according to the poet?
    According to the poet, quality shoes help people walk, dance, run, jump comfortably, and safely return home.
  • What does ‘return home’ symbolise besides the literal act of returning?
    Besides the literal act of coming back, ‘return home’ symbolises safety, comfort, security, and the completion of a fulfilling day's journey.
  • Identify the phrase that tells us that every worker’s contribution is distinct.
    “each celebrating what belongs to them and to none else”
  • Complete the following with suitable words.
    ...for the feet that walk, dance, run, jump, return home refers to ________________.
    people from all walks of life going about their daily activities and journeys.

    • II. Answer the following questions.

    1. Why does the poet say, ‘I hear Bharat celebrating, the varied vocations I hear’?
      The poet says this because the sounds, rhythms, and dedication of workers across different professions bring life, joy, and progress to the nation. Their honest work feels like a collective celebration of India's diversity and spirit.
    2. What does the electrician ‘humming’ while getting ready for work suggest?
      The electrician ‘humming’ suggests enthusiasm, pride, contentment, and a positive attitude toward his work as he prepares to brighten people's lives.
    3. Explain the significance of the line, ‘The voice of their vocation is the voice of their identity.’
      This line signifies that a person’s work is not merely a means to earn a living—it reflects their unique skill, passion, dignity, and role in society, shaping who they truly are.
    4. Do you think the role of all the people belonging to different vocations is important in our daily lives? Support your answer with a reason.
      Yes, every vocation is deeply important in our daily lives because society is interdependent. From farmers who grow our food and masons who build our homes to electricians who power our lives, each worker fulfills an essential need that keeps communities running smoothly.
    5. Why is the poet celebrating all the vocations in the poem? Explain by giving examples from your context.
      The poet celebrates all vocations to promote the dignity of labour and show that no job is superior or inferior. In our daily context, sanitation workers keep our neighbourhoods clean, teachers shape young minds, doctors care for our health, and drivers help us travel safely—each deserves equal respect.
    6. How does the poet use sensory imagery to bring out the beauty of everyday work?
      The poet blends visual imagery (woven colours and myriad hues), auditory imagery (lutes, humming electricians, singing boatmen), and taste-related imagery (“delicious singing of the cook”) to turn ordinary tasks into vibrant, artistic, and joyful experiences.

    Did You Know?

    Accredited Social Health Activist (ASHA) is a group of community health workers employed by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) as a part of India's National Rural Health Mission (NRHM).

    Speaking Activity

    Sample Speech (Vocation: Potter / Kumhar):

    "Good morning everyone! Today, I represent a potter. I work in a small workshop with clay, water, a spinning wheel, and a kiln. Every day, I shape wet clay with my hands to create earthen pots (matkas), diyas, and vessels that keep water cool naturally. While people love my eco-friendly craft, I often face challenges like changing weather affecting clay drying and competition from plastic products. My message to everyone is to respect traditional artisans and support handmade, eco-friendly crafts."

    I. Poem: The Lamplighter (by R.L. Stevenson)

    Summary: In this poem, a child eagerly waits by the window at tea-time to watch Leerie, the lamplighter, walk down the street with his lantern and ladder to light the street lamps. While other children want to be drivers, sailors, or rich bankers, the speaker wishes to grow up and light lamps alongside Leerie at night.
