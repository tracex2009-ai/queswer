1. Reflect and Respond
Q I. Does anyone in your family use a hand fan? What do you call it in your language? What is it made of?
Answer: Yes, my grandparents still use a hand fan during summer or power cuts. In Hindi, we call it "Haath ka Pankha" or "Bijna". It is usually made of bamboo strips, palm leaves, or colorful cloth stitched onto a wooden handle.

3. Critical Reflection
Extract: "Over time, pankhas became significant cultural goods distributed through trade routes. They were considered exotic and stylish. Although there was substantial commonality in their use across India, different villages and towns developed their own varieties of traditional pankhas. Each place developed pankhas with distinct materials or a variety of intricate designs, that set them apart from one another."
(i) State whether the following sentence is true or false: "Pankhas were one of the most popular items of commerce."
Answer: True. (Because they became significant cultural goods distributed widely through trade routes).
(ii) Why has the word ‘traditional’ been used to describe pankhas?
Answer: The word ‘traditional’ is used because pankhas have been crafted and used across generations as part of India's heritage, with each village and town passing down its own unique local designs and techniques.
(iii) Complete the following statement: The sentence ‘They were considered exotic and stylish’ is an opinion and not a fact because ______________.
Answer: ...words like ‘exotic’ and ‘stylish’ reflect personal taste, perception, or judgment rather than something that can be objectively measured or proven.
(iv) Infer one reason for commonality in the use of pankhas across India.
Answer: India mostly has a warm and humid climate, so people across all regions needed a simple, handy tool to keep themselves cool before electricity was invented.
(v) Select the correct assertion for the given Reason:
Reason: Pankhas were made of indigenous materials, unique to the region, with elaborate designs.
Answer: Assertion: Each kind of pankha could be distinguished from the other (different villages and towns developed distinct varieties that set them apart).
