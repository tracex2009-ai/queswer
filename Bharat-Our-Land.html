Poem - Bharat Our Land



Poet: Subramania Bharati
Summary:

In the poem "Bharat Our Land", the poet celebrates India's unmatched natural beauty, glorious history, and deep spiritual heritage. He praises the mighty Himalayas (Himavant), the life-giving river Ganga, and ancient sacred texts like the Upanishads. The poet also honours India's brave warriors, wise sages, divine music, and the spiritual teachings of Brahma-knowledge and Lord Buddha. Through the repeated refrain "she's peerless, let's praise her!", he inspires deep patriotism and pride in every citizen.

II. Work in pairs. Discuss what the words 'जय हे' (Jaya he) in the last two lines of the National Anthem convey. Share your answer with your classmates and teacher.

Answer: The words 'Jaya He' mean "Victory to Thee" or "Glory to You." In the National Anthem, repeating 'Jaya He' expresses deep respect, pride, and wishes for the continuous triumph, peace, and prosperity of our motherland, India.

II. Complete the following features about the poem.

  1. The impact on the readers (mood): Patriotic, inspiring, reverent, and proud
  2. The poet's attitude (tone): Admiring, celebratory, and deeply respectful
  3. The rhyme scheme: AABB (in each four-line stanza)
  4. Examples of personification:
    • "The generous Ganga is ours" (giving the river the human quality of generosity)
    • "She's peerless, let's praise her!" (referring to Bharat as a living woman/motherland worthy of praise)

III. What is the impact of the refrain, 'she's peerless, let's praise her!'?

Answer: The repetition of the refrain "she's peerless, let's praise her!" at the end of every stanza creates a musical, chant-like rhythm. It reinforces the central idea that India is completely unmatched in beauty, wisdom, and heritage, inspiring readers to feel proud and honour their nation.

IV. Complete the following sentence appropriately.

India is metaphorically described as 'this sunny golden land,' suggesting that it is bright, prosperous, fertile, and rich in natural beauty as well as cultural glory.

VI. The poet uses imagery (descriptive language to create vivid mental pictures that appeal to the senses). Give any two examples from the poem.

Answer:

  1. Visual Imagery: "This sunny golden land" — creates a bright, warm mental picture of India's sunlit landscapes and richness.
  2. Visual / Nature Imagery: "The mighty Himavant is ours" and "The generous Ganga is ours" — create vivid pictures of towering snow-covered mountains and a wide, flowing river.

VII. What is the impact of the use of hyphens in the first stanza? Select the options that are true.

Correct Options:

  • 1. Creates deliberate pauses to reinforce the sense of admiration and pride
  • 3. Lends to the lyrical quality giving it a measured, chant-like rhythm
  • 5. Presents a key element of India's greatness before the hyphen and makes a strong assertion about its uniqueness after the hyphen

IX. Now, identify examples from the poem that correspond to it being an ode.

Answer:

  • Direct Praise and Reverence: Lines like "She's peerless, let's praise her!" and "Let's praise her!" show deep admiration and celebration of the motherland.
  • Grand and Elevated Language: Phrases such as "mighty Himavant," "generous Ganga," "sacred Upanishads," and "hoary antiquity" give the poem a noble, formal, and majestic tone typical of an ode.

X. Read the explanations and identify the matching allusions from the poem for the following.

  1. An allusion to the ancient Indian scriptures that form the philosophical core and symbolise deep wisdom and enlightenment:
    Answer: The sacred Upanishads ("The sacred Upanishads are ours")
  2. An allusion to the concept of spiritual knowledge associated with self-realisation and ultimate truth:
    Answer: Brahma-knowledge ("Brahma-knowledge has taken root")
  3. An allusion to Gautama Buddha's teachings, which include compassion, non-violence, and enlightenment:
    Answer: Buddha's Dhamma / law ("And the Buddha preached his dhamma here")

Critical Reflection


I.Read the extract given below and answer the question that follow

The Mighty Himavant is ours-
there's no equal anywhere on earth
The generous Ganga is ours-
which other river can match her grace?
The sacred Upanishads are ours-
what scriptures else to name with them?

1. Complete the sentence.

Question:

The word 'mighty' refers to the _______ of the Himavant.

Answer :
The word 'mighty' refers to the great strength, height, and grandeur of the Himavant.


2. Choose the correct option to complete the sentence given below.

Question:

The question mark at the end of the fourth and sixth line of the extract is used to __________.

Options:

(i) show doubt and uncertainty
(ii) emphasise a point
(iii) highlight confusion and hesitation
(iv) reveal inquiry and curiosity

Answer :
(ii) emphasise a point


3. The poet uses the word 'generous' to describe the Ganga. Why?

Answer :
The poet calls the Ganga 'generous' because it selflessly provides water, life, and fertility to the land and its people.


4. Why has the movement of the river been described as graceful?

Answer :
The river's movement is described as graceful because it flows smoothly and majestically symbolizing purity and natural beauty.


5. Fill in the blank with the correct option from the words given in the brackets.

The poet is implying that the Upanishads are _________(unmatched/abundant) in their wisdom and spiritual depth.

Answer :
unmatched


II. Answer the following questions.

1. How does the poem reflect a strong connection to cultural identity and heritage?

Answer :
The poem celebrates India's sacred rivers, mighty mountains, ancient scriptures like the Upanishads, brave warriors, and wise sages, which together form India's rich cultural and spiritual heritage.


2. What can you infer about the poet's attitude towards India from the repeated phrase "she's peerless, let's praise her!"?

Answer :
The repeated phrase shows the poet's deep love, admiration, and immense pride for his motherland, which he believes has no equal in the entire world.


3. What does the line 'many a sage has sanctified this land' suggest about India's spiritual heritage?

Answer :
This line suggests that India has been blessed by great saints and spiritual thinkers whose wisdom, teachings, and spiritual knowledge have made the land pure and sacred.


4. How does the poet connect warriors and music to India's greatness?

Answer :
The poet highlights that India is a land of both bravery and fine arts, where gallant warriors protect the nation with courage and divine music enriches its soul and culture.


5. How does this poem foster a sense of national pride?

Answer :
By reminding readers of India’s unmatched natural beauty, glorious history, bravery, and deep spiritual wisdom, the poem inspires respect, unity, and patriotism among citizens.

Writing Task

I. Write a paragraph about a place that you have visited which has stayed in your memory.

A Memorable Trip to Rishikesh

Last summer, I visited Rishikesh in Uttarakhand with my parents and younger sister to take a refreshing break from our busy school routine. Before the trip, we packed light cotton clothes, comfortable walking shoes, a first-aid kit, and a camera to capture the scenic views. We stayed in a peaceful riverside cottage surrounded by green hills and the soothing sound of the flowing Ganga. During our three-day stay, we walked across the famous Lakshman Jhula, tried safe river rafting with trained guides, visited ancient temples, and attended the magical evening Ganga Aarti at Parmarth Niketan. The cool mountain breeze, glowing diyas floating on the river, and peaceful atmosphere made this trip an unforgettable memory that still brings a smile to my face.

II. Interdisciplinary Project: India's Major Geographical Features

Geographical Feature Example & Region Importance in People's Lives Languages Spoken
1. Mountains The Himalayas (Northern India) Act as a natural barrier against cold winds, provide perennial rivers, and support tourism, forestry, and terrace farming. Hindi, Kashmiri, Dogri, Nepali, Garhwali, Kumaoni
