Check Your Understanding (Part I)

I. Do you think pot making is easy? If yes, why? If no, why not?

Answer: No, pot making is not easy. It requires hard physical labour, patience, and skill—from digging and soaking the clay, pounding it inside bamboo cylinders into soft dough, to shaping it delicately without letting it crack or collapse, and finally firing it properly in a kiln.

II. Would Sentila be able to fulfil her dream of becoming a pot maker? Explain.

Answer: Yes, Sentila would be able to fulfil her dream because she is deeply passionate, observant, and determined. Even though her mother initially wanted her to learn weaving instead, Sentila never gave up and kept practising until she mastered the craft.

III. Do you think Mesoba and Arenla would support Sentila? Give a reason.

Answer: Yes, Mesoba and Arenla would eventually support Sentila. Mesoba already understood that traditional skills belong to the whole community and must be passed on to anyone eager to learn, while Arenla only hesitated out of concern for Sentila's hardship and low income—not out of lack of love.

Check Your Understanding (Part II)

I. Do you think Onula's support helped Sentila? If yes, why? If no, why not?

Answer: Yes, Onula's support helped Sentila tremendously. When Sentila was tense and struggling to shape the clay, Onula gently encouraged her, demonstrated the right technique with patience, and restored her confidence so she could finally make beautiful pots.

II. Sentila observes her mother making pots. What does this tell us about her?

Answer: This tells us that Sentila is keen, attentive, and genuinely dedicated to learning pottery. Instead of giving up after early failures, she carefully watches every step to understand the finer details of the craft.

Critical Reflection

I. Read the extracts given below and answer the questions that follow.

Extract 1

"Pounding the stubborn clay inside bamboo cylinders to soften it, is also tedious. So many times I've dropped the mould out of sheer exhaustion and have had to start all over again. It takes months to bring out a batch of pots after so much labour. And the reward? A few rupees. But if Sentila learns weaving, she can make much more money besides providing enough cloth for the family. Weaving is not messy like pot making and can be done indoors in all seasons. Also, the time spent on weaving one shawl is much less and the return is handsome."

(i) Choose the correct reason for the given assertion.
(A): The effort in making pots is far greater than the returns.
Answer: A. The process of pot making is quite tiresome and long, and one hardly earns much.

(ii) Why does Arenla want Sentila to learn weaving?
Answer: Arenla wants Sentila to learn weaving because it is cleaner, takes less time, can be done indoors in any weather, provides cloth for the family, and brings much higher earnings than pot making.

(iii) State one advantage that weaving has over pot making, as per the extract.
Answer: Weaving is not messy like pot making and can be done indoors during all seasons.

(iv) Choose the sentence that uses the word 'handsome' in the same way as in the extract.
Answer: B. They will make a handsome profit selling this property.

(v) 'And the reward?' What is the author's purpose of using a question mark here?
Answer: The author uses a rhetorical question mark to highlight the contrast between the extreme hard work of pot making and the disappointingly small amount of money earned in return.

Extract 2

"Onula saw her taking out some clay and the implements from her basket quietly. She watched Sentila's clumsy efforts to make a pot and noticed that Sentila was too tense. As a result, the clay seemed unable or unwilling to yield the right shape. When Sentila wearily let the misshapen lump fall flat on the ground, Onula went to her and said, 'Don't worry, little one, I shall teach you how to make a perfect pot.' Sentila watched in amazement as Onula fashioned a beautiful pot and asked her to try again."

(i) Complete the sentence with an appropriate reason.
Onula feels Sentila's effort at making a pot is clumsy because Sentila was too tense and nervous while handling the clay.

(ii) Choose the correct option to complete the following sentence.
'Don't worry, little one, I shall teach you how to make a perfect pot.' This shows that Onula was ______.
Answer: C. thoughtful and generous

(iii) Which among the following is the effect of a cause?
Answer: A. As a result, the clay seemed unable or unwilling to yield the right shape.

(iv) 'Onula fashioned a beautiful pot.' Here, the word 'fashioned' means ______. (created/styled)
Answer: created

(v) How might Sentila have felt when she saw 'the misshapen lump fall flat on the ground'?
Answer: Sentila must have felt deeply disappointed, frustrated, and disheartened after trying so hard and failing once again.

II. Answer the following questions.

1. Describe the process of pot making followed by expert pot makers, as observed by Sentila.
Answer: Expert pot makers dig grey and red clay from the riverbank, soak it in a trough, and pound it inside bamboo cylinders to make soft, malleable dough. They then shape the clay smoothly with wet hands and simple wooden implements, dry the pots in the sun, and bake them carefully in a kiln on a bed of hay.

2. What warning was given to Mesoba by the village council?
Answer: The village council warned Mesoba that traditional crafts like pottery belong to the entire community and not to any single individual, so Arenla was obliged to pass on her skill to anyone who wished to learn—especially her own daughter.

3. How did Sentila feel when she failed at pot making even after a year of training with her mother?
Answer: Sentila felt discouraged, frustrated, and anxious because despite a full year of hard practice, her pots still turned out misshapen while her mother wanted her to switch to weaving.

4. 'Onula stood there for a long time as if trying to absorb a new phenomenon'. Explain.
Answer: When Onula saw two neat rows of freshly made pots inside the work shed, she realized that Arenla could not have made them all alone while unwell. She stood amazed as it sank in that young Sentila had finally mastered the art of pot making.

5. 'The tradition and history of the people did not belong to any individual,' what does this symbolise?
Answer: This symbolises that cultural heritage and traditional knowledge are collective treasures of a community. Preserving them requires sharing skills freely with the younger generation rather than keeping them restricted.

6. What is the significance of the concluding line of the story, 'A new pot maker was born'?
Answer: The concluding line marks Sentila's transformation from a struggling learner into a true artisan. It celebrates her perseverance and ensures that the village's ancient pottery tradition will continue to live on.

7. What is the role of perseverance in pursuing one's dreams? Elaborate with reference to Sentila.
Answer: Perseverance helps a person overcome self-doubt, failure, and obstacles on the path to success. Sentila faced her mother's reluctance and failed for an entire year, yet her quiet determination and continuous practice under Onula's guidance finally turned her dream into reality.

II. Review of the story 'Quality' by John Galsworthy (Traditional Crafts vs. Industrialisation)

Book / Story Review: 'Quality' by John Galsworthy

Introduction: 'Quality' is a touching short story by John Galsworthy that highlights the struggle of traditional craftsmen against modern factory-made goods. The story revolves around the Gessler brothers, two German shoemakers living in London who treat shoemaking as a sacred art rather than just a business.

Main Theme – Craftsmanship vs. Mass Production: The Gessler brothers use only the finest leather and stitch every pair of boots by hand with immense patience, pride, and perfection. However, big industrial companies capture the market through flashy advertisements and cheap, mass-produced footwear. Because the Gesslers refuse to compromise on quality or speed up their work using machines, they slowly lose customers and face extreme poverty.

Message & Connection to 'The Pot Maker': Like Arenla and Sentila in 'The Pot Maker', the Gessler brothers show deep devotion to their traditional craft. The story reminds us that while industrialisation brings speed and profit, it often destroys authentic human artistry and the livelihoods of skilled artisans. Supporting local handicrafts is essential to keep our cultural heritage alive.
